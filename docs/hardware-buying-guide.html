1What the software expects from your hardware

Your device only has to do one thing: send a small JSON message to the BahaAI server. Everything else — alerts, predictions, maps, notifications — already works.

FieldRuleWhat it means for your hardware
water_levelRequired · number ≥ 0Water height in metres. This is the only field you must send. It comes from the ultrasonic sensor.
rainfallOptional · number ≥ 0Millimetres of rain. See the honest warning in Section 4.
temperatureOptional · numberDegrees Celsius — from the DHT22.
humidityOptional · 0 to 100Percent — from the same DHT22.
recorded_atOptional · dateLeave blank and the server timestamps it on arrival.

Four rules that decide what you buy — do not skip these.

1. The sensor measures DOWN, the app wants UP. An ultrasonic sensor on a post reports the distance to the water. The app wants the water level. Your code must do level = mount_height − measured_distance. Get this backwards and every alert fires inverted.

2. Minimum range is 25 cm. The waterproof ultrasonic sensor is blind closer than 25 cm. You cannot mount it just above the water — leave at least 25 cm of air above the highest water you expect. Design your test tank around this.

3. Send a reading at least every 10 minutes. The system marks a sensor "offline" after 30 minutes of silence and raises a fault alert. Use a 5-minute interval.

4. The prediction needs at least 3 readings within 2 hours. At 5-minute intervals you get 24 readings — comfortable. Any slower and the AI prediction silently returns nothing.

You only need ONE working sensor. The system handles 7 flood zones, but each is independent. One real device plus the built-in simulator (php artisan sensors:simulate) demonstrates the entire system. Do not buy seven of everything.

For the desk, indoors, on a breadboard. This is where students write and debug the firmware before anything gets soldered or gets wet. Subtotal ≈ ₱1,444 (₱1,794 with the multimeter).

4The one honest compromise — rainfall

Rainfall is the only field where the cheap part cannot do the real job. Decide this deliberately and write your decision into the paper.

The ₱75 rain module cannot measure millimetres. It detects whether its surface is wet or dry. If you feed its output into the rainfall field, you are publishing an invented number — and a panel member who knows sensors will ask. Use it only as a "raining right now" indicator, and say so explicitly.

OptionCostTrade-off
A. Use the weather API
Recommended		 ₱0 BahaAI already pulls rainfall for Consolacion from OpenWeatherMap. Leave rainfall empty on the device and let the weather service supply it. Defend it as deliberate separation of data sources — water level from your sensor, meteorology from a meteorological service. Costs nothing and is fully honest.
B. Build a tipping bucket
Best for marks		 ≈ ₱150 A reed switch (₱30) plus a magnet and a small see-saw bucket you 3D-print or cut from a bottle. Calibrate by pouring a measured volume and counting tips. Real millimetres, and it is an original contribution you can write a whole chapter about.
C. Buy a tipping bucket ₱1,200 – 2,500 Accurate and finished, but it is the most expensive single item on this list — for a field you marked optional. Only justified if the study is specifically about rainfall measurement.
D. ₱75 wet/dry module ₱75 Indicator only. Fine as a supplementary "it is raining" flag. Never send it as a millimetre value.

9Two software changes needed before hardware works

1. The reading endpoint has no authentication. POST /api/sensors/{id}/readings is currently open — anyone who knows the address can inject fake readings and trigger real alerts to real residents. Acceptable on a closed bench network; add a Sanctum API token before this touches a public network, and document the mitigation in the paper.

2. Devices cannot report their battery level. The sensors table has a battery_level column and the dashboard displays it, but it is not accepted by the reading endpoint — so a real node has no way to send it. If you want that panel to show live data, add battery_level to the request validation rules and to the API controller.
