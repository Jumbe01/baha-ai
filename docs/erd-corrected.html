How this was produced

Every table, column, type and foreign key below was read directly out of the schema after running all migrations. It is not a redrawing of the old diagram — it is the database describing itself, so it cannot disagree with the implementation.

1What was wrong with the previous ERD

The earlier diagram covered 9 entities. The delivered database has 16 tables. These are the differences that matter.

IssuePrevious ERDActual implementation
Units were wrong WaterLevelCM, RainFallAmount_cm, PredictedLevelCM Water level and predicted level are stored in metres; rainfall in millimetres. The zone thresholds of 1.5 and 3.0 confirm it — as centimetres a critical flood would be 3 cm deep.
AlertRecipient attributes did not exist DeliveryStatus, DispatchChannel on AlertRecipient Those belong to notification_logs, a separate table. alert_recipients holds only the inbox state (read_at). The two were merged in error.
Zone was missing its thresholds Only ZoneName, RiskLevel, Population safe_threshold, warning_threshold and critical_threshold drive every alert and actuation decision in the system, and none appeared.
Population does not exist Population on Zone No such column. Affected-resident counts are derived at incident time from residents registered in the barangay.
Attributes with no column InstallDate, InstalledDate, SentAt, RiskStatus, WeatherCondition None exist. Risk is computed at request time from the zone thresholds rather than stored, so it can never fall out of date when a threshold is edited.
Seven tables absent — notification_logs, actuator_logs, audit_logs, flood_incidents, flood_reports, weather_data, otp_codes
Two relationships missing Alert linked only to Zone; FloodPrediction linked only to Zone Both also reference the sensor that produced them.

One thing the previous ERD got right

It showed ActuatorUnit related to Zone as many-to-one, which is exactly how the system works. The manuscript narrative accompanying the ERD says instead that a sensor may be paired one-to-one with an actuator unit — that sentence is the error, not the diagram. Actuators belong to a zone, which is also how DRRMO equipment is deployed in practice.

4Design decisions worth defending

Four places where the implementation deliberately differs from the earlier design. Each has a reason, and each is short enough to state in one sentence if asked.

DecisionReason
Risk is computed, not stored A stored RiskStatus on each reading would become wrong the moment an administrator edited a zone threshold, leaving historical rows classified against rules that no longer exist. Risk is derived on demand from the reading and the current thresholds, so it is always consistent.
Rainfall lives on the reading The earlier design had a separate Rainfall Record entity. One field node reports water level and rainfall in the same transmission at the same instant, so splitting them would double the row count and require a join to reunite values that were never apart.
Inbox and delivery audit are separate alert_recipients answers "has this person seen it?" and notification_logs answers "what did we attempt to send, through which channel, and what happened?" One person can generate several delivery rows for a single alert, and a siren generates a delivery row with no person at all. They are different questions with different cardinalities.
Actuators belong to a zone A pump serves an area, not an instrument. Tying equipment one-to-one to a sensor would mean a zone with two sensors could not share one pump, and a failed sensor would orphan working equipment.

Units — state these explicitly in the manuscript

Water level, predicted level and all thresholds are in metres. Rainfall is in millimetres. Rate of rise is in metres per minute. Confidence is R² expressed as a percentage, which measures goodness of fit to a straight line, not the probability that the forecast is correct.
